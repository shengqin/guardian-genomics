01 — The gap

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death, and today's risk tools still miss people.

Clinical calculators

Age, cholesterol, blood pressure

Equations such as PREVENT estimate risk from a handful of clinical factors.

Many patients with disease are rated low or borderline risk.

Polygenic scores

Inherited DNA variants

They capture genetics, but they are fixed at birth and were built mostly in European-ancestry cohorts.

They can't tell whether risk genes are actually active.

Imaging

Calcium scans and angiography

Imaging shows plaque directly, but it requires a separate visit and radiation exposure.

It sees disease once structure has changed.

Our approach

CardioGuardian reads the activity of the genes that drive inherited cardiovascular risk, from a single routine blood draw.

02 — The science

Genetics chooses the genes. Cell-free RNA measures them.

1

Find the genes that matter

We link cardiovascular GWAS risk variants to the genes they regulate in artery tissue, using a regulatory trait concordance method applied to GTEx eQTL maps of coronary artery and aorta.

GWAS × eQTL
2

Measure them in blood

Cells in the artery wall and immune system shed RNA into the bloodstream. From one plasma sample, we sequence the cell-free RNA of those risk genes.

Plasma cfRNA
3

Score the risk

Each gene's expression is weighted by its genetic effect, producing a single CardioGuardian score designed to complement clinical risk equations.

One score
score = Σ expression × genetic effect
Genetically informed, expression-based, and measured from a routine blood draw.

Finding 01

The CardioGuardian score was significantly higher in patients with cardiovascular disease than in those without.

Finding 02

It outperformed a polygenic risk score in the same cohort and added information beyond the PREVENT clinical risk score.

Finding 03

It flagged patients with disease even when their clinical risk was rated low or borderline.

What's next

We are expanding independent validation in additional patient cohorts, toward 500 samples, and moving from exome-wide sequencing to a compact targeted panel already designed for lab transfer.

Results from a cross-sectional cohort; manuscript currently in peer review. CardioGuardian is an investigational research assay. It has not been cleared or approved by the U.S. FDA and is not available for clinical use.
